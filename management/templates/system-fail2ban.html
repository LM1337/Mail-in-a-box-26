Intrusion Protection

fail2ban watches the logs of this box (mail, webmail, Nextcloud, ActiveSync, this control panel) and bans IP addresses that fail to log in too often. On top of Mail-in-a-Box’s own rules (20 failures within 30 seconds, banned for 10 minutes), repeat offenders get longer bans on the mail ports each time, slow jails catch attacks that try only a few passwords per hour, and addresses on the allowlist are never banned.

Address Banned in, and why

“Unban” releases the address in all jails, recidive included, and deletes its ban history, so a new ban starts again at the initial ban time. “in this jail only” releases only that jail’s ban. Failures that other jails counted but did not ban yet stay counted: if a device keeps getting banned, fix its password or put its network on the allowlist below.

Nextcloud throttle

Nextcloud has a throttle of its own, apart from fail2ban. After failed logins from an address (within the last 12 hours) it delays every login from there, up to 25 seconds, and after more than 10 failures within 30 minutes it refuses them with HTTP 429 (“Too many requests”). This hits every account behind that address, also those with the right password: one calendar or contacts app with an old password keeps the other devices of its network from syncing. A successful login removes only the failures of that account.

Settings

Each new ban of an address in a jail lasts twice as long as its previous ban in that jail, up to the maximum ban time, and the address is banned again after fewer failures there. Bans of the web ports (control panel, webmail, Nextcloud, ActiveSync) keep their fixed length. Addresses banned again and again in any jail get the one-week recidive ban on all ports, as before. fail2ban keeps the ban history for the memory period, also across restarts.

Additional jails with a long window for SMTP AUTH and IMAP/POP3/ManageSieve. They block only the mail ports, so webmail and this control panel stay reachable for a banned address. They count the failures of an address, not of an account: all devices behind one office address add up, so put that address on the allowlist.

The same for Roundcube, Nextcloud, ActiveSync (Exchange accounts of phones, and Autodiscover) and this control panel, with the same thresholds. They block HTTP and HTTPS: a calendar or contacts app or a phone with an old password gets its whole network banned from webmail, Nextcloud, ActiveSync and this control panel (for the first ban time; these bans do not grow). Calendar and contacts apps that use the short paths /caldav, /carddav and /webdav are counted under their own address now, as on /cloud; before, these paths reached Nextcloud from the box itself and were never banned. Turn them on only with your own networks on the allowlist.

Thresholds of Mail-in-a-Box’s jails

Failed logins of one address within the window that lead to a ban, and how long the ban lasts (for repeat offenders on the mail ports the first ban, if adaptive banning is on). Leave a field at its default to follow Mail-in-a-Box’s value. recidive is not listed: it bans addresses that these jails banned again and again.

JailFailuresWithinBan timeWatches

Nextcloud’s own throttle, see above. Turned off, only the Nextcloud jail of fail2ban stays (20 failures within 2 minutes), and passwords can be tried on Nextcloud, CalDAV and CardDAV as fast as the server answers.

The addresses and networks of the allowlist above go on Nextcloud’s bypass list too, so Nextcloud never delays or refuses them. Only for networks of your own: an address that you share with others (mobile networks, satellite and other carrier-grade NAT) would be exempt for them as well.

Durations are in seconds, or with a unit: 30m, 6h, 2d, 1w. With adaptive banning and the slow jails off, an empty allowlist and the jails’ default thresholds, fail2ban runs exactly with Mail-in-a-Box’s original configuration.
